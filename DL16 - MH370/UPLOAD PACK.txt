Description:
On March 8, 2014, Malaysia Airlines Flight 370 (a Boeing 777-200ER carrying 239 passengers and crew) departed Kuala Lumpur International Airport at 00:42 MYT bound for Beijing—and became the greatest aviation mystery of the modern era.

- Transponder cutoff: Right at the border between Malaysian and Vietnamese airspace (01:21 MYT), the aircraft's transponder stopped transmitting, cutting secondary civilian radar contact.
- The deliberate U-turn: Malaysian military primary radar tracked the jet making a sharp turn back southwest across the Malay Peninsula and up the Strait of Malacca, with zero distress calls.
- The 7 silent pings: For roughly six more hours after vanishing from civilian radar, an Inmarsat satellite over the Indian Ocean recorded seven automated hourly electronic "handshakes" with the aircraft, ending along the remote "7th Arc" in the southern Indian Ocean.
- The search & debris: Ships and autonomous submersibles searched over 120,000 square kilometers of deep ocean floor in the most expensive aviation search in history. Beginning in July 2015 on Réunion Island, barnacle-covered wing fragments (including the right flaperon) washed ashore thousands of miles away along the coast of Africa and Indian Ocean islands—yet the main fuselage and black boxes were never found.

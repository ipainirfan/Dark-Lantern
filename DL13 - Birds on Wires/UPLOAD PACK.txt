SOURCES
- Birds on a single wire: no potential difference across the body, so negligible current flows (standard physics; e.g. BBC Science Focus, "Why don't birds get electrocuted while perching on power lines?").
- Electrocution happens when a bird touches two wires, or a wire and a grounded part, at once; up to 11.6 million birds/year may be killed by electrocution on US power lines (Loss et al. 2014, PLOS ONE, PMC4081594); 333 of 417 electrocuted raptors examined by the US Fish & Wildlife Service forensics lab (2000–2015) were bald or golden eagles – National Wildlife Federation, "Saving eagles from the lethal hazards of power line electrocution" (2021).
- Downed power lines: stay away and call the utility/emergency services; touching a line while grounded makes your body the path to ground (standard utility safety guidance).

Description:
Between the Indonesian islands of Bali and Lombok lies the Lombok Strait—a narrow channel of water just 20 miles (35 km) wide. Yet it marks one of the sharpest biological boundaries on Earth: the Wallace Line.

- West of the line (Bali, Java, Borneo, Sumatra, Malaysia): The jungles are home to Asian placental mammals—tigers, monkeys, rhinos, and elephants.
- East of the line (Lombok, Sulawesi, and toward Australia): Asian mammals vanish, replaced by Australasian lineages including marsupials, tree kangaroos, and cockatoos. Even many bird species that could physically fly across the 20-mile strait stay strictly on their own side.
- The Ice Age secret: During the Pleistocene Ice Ages, global sea levels dropped by over 300 feet (~120 meters), exposing the shallow Sunda Shelf and creating a continuous dry-land bridge from mainland Asia all the way to Bali.
- The unbroken trench: Right between Bali and Lombok, the ocean floor plunges into a deep tectonic trench over 1,000 feet deep that never dried up—keeping the two evolutionary worlds separated for millions of years.

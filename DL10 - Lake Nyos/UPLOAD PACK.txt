Description:
Can a lake explode without fire or flood? In 1986, Lake Nyos in Cameroon silently released a massive cloud of trapped volcanic CO2. Because carbon dioxide is heavier than air, the invisible gas cloud rolled down the valleys and suffocated 1,746 people in their sleep up to 25 km away.

Today, degassing pipes vent Lake Nyos so it can't happen again—but two other lakes in Africa (Lake Monoun and Lake Kivu) still hold deadly volcanic gas like this.

Sources:
- Lake Nyos limnic eruption, 21 Aug 1986 (~1,746 people and ~3,500 livestock killed, up to ~25 km away) – USGS / Britannica
- CO2 from magma beneath the lake; denser-than-air gas cloud – USGS
- Degassing pipes installed from 2001 – Kling et al. / Nyos degassing project

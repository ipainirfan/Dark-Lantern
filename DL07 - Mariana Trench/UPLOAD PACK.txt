Why Don't We Go to the Bottom of the Ocean? 🌊 #shorts

Description:
The deepest place on Earth is 11 km down – drop Mount Everest in and its peak would still be 2 km underwater. So why has almost nobody been there?

🌑 No sunlight below ~1 km
🥶 Water just above freezing
🐘 Over 1,000x the pressure at the surface – like an elephant standing on your thumb
🚢 1960: two men reached the bottom in the Trieste – a window cracked on the way down. Nobody returned for 52 years.
🛍️ Scientists have filmed a plastic bag almost 11 km down, and tiny creatures living there carry our pollution in their bodies.

Only a few dozen people have ever reached the bottom. Our trash is already living there.

SOURCES (fact check)
- Challenger Deep ~10,935 m deep; Everest 8,849 m (difference ~2 km) – NOAA / Wikipedia "Mariana Trench".
- Pressure >1,000 times surface pressure (~1,086 bar, about 8 tons per square inch) – NOAA Ocean Explorer.
- No sunlight below ~1,000 m (midnight zone); bottom water ~1–4 °C – NOAA.
- 23 Jan 1960 Trieste dive, Jacques Piccard & Don Walsh: outer window cracked during descent, ~20 min on the bottom, view clouded by silt.
- Next crewed visit: James Cameron, 2012 (52 years later).
- Plastic bag at 10,898 m: Chiba et al., Marine Policy (2018), Deep-sea Debris Database.
- Amphipods with PCB/PBDE pollutants in the Mariana Trench: Jamieson et al., Nature Ecology & Evolution (2017).

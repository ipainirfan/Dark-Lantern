Description:
On August 27, 1883, the Krakatoa volcano in Indonesia blew itself apart in one of the deadliest eruptions in recorded history, killing over 36,000 people.

- 64 km away: The blast wave ruptured the eardrums of sailors aboard the British ship Norham Castle.
- 160 km away: A gasometer pressure gauge in Batavia spiked off the scale at an estimated 172 decibels—louder than standing next to a jet engine.
- 4,800 km away: On Rodrigues Island across the Indian Ocean, police heard the roar and mistook it for distant cannon fire.
- Around the globe: Barometers worldwide recorded the atmospheric pressure wave circling the Earth at least 3 times (up to 4 times on sensitive instruments).

Two-thirds of the island vanished, volcanic ash turned sunsets blood red around the world for months, and in 1927 a new volcano—Anak Krakatau ("Child of Krakatoa")—rose from the water in the exact same spot. It is still erupting today.

Description:
Fall into a black hole and the people watching would never see you go in. Your image would slow down, turn red and freeze at the edge… forever.

How do we know black holes are real?
🕯️ 1916 – Karl Schwarzschild solved Einstein's equations from the trenches of World War One and found gravity that traps light. He died months later.
🌌 2015 – LIGO detectors felt the ripple of two black holes colliding 1.3 billion light years away (Nobel Prize 2017).
📸 2019 – The Event Horizon Telescope took the first photo of a black hole, M87*, 6.5 billion times heavier than our Sun.
🍝 2019 – Telescopes watched a star get stretched into a stream as it fell into a black hole (event AT2019qiz) – real spaghettification.

SOURCES (fact check)
- Schwarzschild solution: sent to Einstein Dec 1915, published 1916; Schwarzschild died 11 May 1916 (illness contracted at the Russian front).
- LIGO GW150914, detected 14 Sept 2015, ~1.3 billion light years (Abbott et al., PRL 2016); Nobel Prize in Physics 2017.
- Event Horizon Telescope, M87* image released 10 April 2019; mass ~6.5 billion suns.
- AT2019qiz tidal disruption event, discovered 2019, reported by ESO Oct 2020 (Nicholl et al., MNRAS 2020).
- Distant observers see infalling objects slow, redshift and fade at the horizon (general relativity).
